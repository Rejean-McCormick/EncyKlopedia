# Semantic boundary — Educational policy

## Subject and territory

The subject is **educational public policy**, including normative goals, institutional governance, resources, access, standards, implementation and evaluated outcomes. It is not the entire field of education. The UNESCO `mt1.10` list is the initial controlled entry set, not an exhaustive subject universe.

## Owned knowledge

- Carefully disambiguated education-policy concepts and their attributed definitions.
- Rights, duties, policy aims and applicable frameworks **with jurisdiction/date**.
- Responsible actors and their actual decisions, institutions, accountability and implementation arrangements.
- Policy mechanisms, financing, targeting, monitoring and evidence-qualified outcomes.
- Historical developments, causal theories, empirical findings and disputes **with provenance and uncertainty**.

## Excluded knowledge

- Detailed pedagogy and cognitive science except when a policy decision relies on them; link to an appropriate branch.
- Whole constitutional/legal systems, broader economic policy and unrelated public finance.
- Full biographies, school histories, institution catalogues or unbounded country profiles.
- Universal statements inferred from one country, one period, one indicator or one interpretation.
- Claims promoted solely from UNESCO labels, candidate Wikidata matches, AI-generated text or undocumented summaries.

## Relationships to other Kristals

The eventual **UNESCO root/index Kristal** owns branch discovery and stable references, not duplicated claims. `unesco-mt1-10` owns only its declared policy domain. Preserve existing UNESCO broader/narrower/related edges, including out-of-branch targets, by stable URI/ID. Other Kristals remain canonical owners for their material. Use explicit semantic cross-references; do not generate new identity equivalences from lexical similarity.

## Native shape and ownership policy

The knowledge shape is a **multi-layer, provenance-bearing policy graph** (concepts, policy instruments, actors, claims, source evidence, jurisdictions and time), with native event and measurement records where necessary. A simple SKOS tree cannot encode policy applicability or a disputed causal effect. Keep lexical mapping, policy descriptions, statistical observations, interpretations and editorial judgments separate.
